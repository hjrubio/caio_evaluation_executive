## 1. Consolidated definition
A Chief AI Officer is the executive accountable for turning AI into business value across the enterprise — **bridging business strategy and AI** [01][02] — while owning the **governance, risk and ethics** of its use [01], and, in its mature form, **operating AI reliably and safely at scale** with clear decision rights [03].

## 2. How the sources fit together
| Dimension | IBM [01] | Wikipedia [02] | CIO.com [03] |

## 3. Points of agreement
1. **Hybrid profile** — technical fluency + executive acumen + ethics are all required [01][02].
2. **Governance is core, not optional** — responsible AI, bias, privacy, security and compliance [01][03].
3. **Cross-functional by nature** — overlaps CIO, CTO, CDO, CISO, COO; reporting lines vary [01][03].
4. **No universal org model** — placement depends on the existing structure [01][03].
5. **Data foundation matters** — the CAIO builds on data infrastructure owned by others [01][03].

## 4. Tensions and evolutions
- **Vision vs. operations:** IBM's 2024 framing stresses strategy and advocacy; the 2026 view says that's CAIO 1.0 and the job is now execution, metrics and guardrails [01 vs 03].
- **Spokesperson vs. steward:** public evangelism was a headline duty in 2024 [01]; by 2026 internal coordination and trade-offs dominate [03].
- **Permanent role vs. transitional role:** IBM treats the rise as inevitable [01]; Heuer suggests success may make the title redundant while the capability persists [03].
- **Optimize vs. transform:** Wikipedia's practitioner view (via Dice) has AI reshaping strategy itself [02], beyond the efficiency gains emphasized elsewhere.

## 5. CAIO Competency Framework (derived)
Seven dimensions combining all three sources. Use these as the shared vocabulary for evaluator personas.

| # | Dimension | What "good" looks like | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | **Strategy & value** | AI strategy tied to business goals; prioritized use cases; may reshape strategy, not only optimize it | 01, 02 |
| D2 | **Operating model & decision rights** | Clear owner for execution, risk and outcomes; defined interfaces with CIO/CTO/CDO/CISO/COO; avoids fragmentation | 03 |
| D3 | **Technology & production delivery** | Right tools/platforms; path from pilot to production workflows; shared models/data/platform standards | 01, 03 |
| D4 | **Governance, risk & ethics** | Responsible-AI policy, bias, privacy, security, regulatory compliance, guardrails | 01, 02, 03 |
| D5 | **Measurement & economics** | ROI/impact metrics; cost awareness (usage-based pricing, infrastructure) | 03 |
| D6 | **People, talent & culture** | Team building, talent attraction, vendor partnerships, change and cultural leadership | 01, 02 |
| D7 | **Communication & advocacy** | Compelling narrative for executives, employees and external stakeholders | 01 |

## 6. Maturity scale (from the 1.0 → 2.0 model)
| Level | Label | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Signal** | AI leadership exists mainly as intent; vague mandate |
| 2 | **Explorer (CAIO 1.0)** | Pilots, PoCs, education; success measured by activity |
| 3 | **Transitioning** | Some production use; standards emerging; partial metrics |
| 4 | **Orchestrator (CAIO 2.0)** | AI embedded in workflows; guardrails; ROI tracked; enterprise coordination |
| 5 | **Embedded** | AI is a routine, governed capability; title matters less than the structures in place |

## 7. Core evaluation questions
Derived mainly from [03]'s accountability questions, extended with [01][02]:
1. Who **owns AI execution**, and are decision rights explicit?
2. Who **manages AI risk**, and what guardrails exist?
3. How do AI investments become **measurable business outcomes**?
4. Is the strategy **aligned with business goals** — or does it reshape them?
5. Does the plan move from **pilots to production**, or stop at experimentation?
6. How does the CAIO **coordinate** with CIO, CTO, CDO, CISO and COO without duplication?
7. Is there a **people and culture** plan (talent, upskilling, change management)?
8. Is the **cost** of AI (infrastructure, usage pricing) accounted for?

## 8. Red flags in a CAIO final project
- Only pilots/PoCs with no path to production (stuck at CAIO 1.0).
- No named owner or decision rights; AI spread across departments with separate standards.
- Governance mentioned as an afterthought or missing bias/privacy/security/compliance.
- No ROI or impact metrics; no cost model.
- Purely technical plan with no business case, stakeholder buy-in or change management.
- Org chart ignores overlap with CIO/CTO/CDO/CISO/COO.

## 10. Source limitations
- Only one quantitative data point across all sources (LinkedIn: CAIO count nearly tripled in five years, as of Apr 2024).
- IBM is a vendor explainer; CIO.com is an opinion piece by an automation-vendor executive; Wikipedia is a stub.
- None covers specific regulations (e.g., EU AI Act) or public-sector CAIO mandates in detail — consider adding sources if personas need regulatory depth.
